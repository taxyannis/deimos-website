# Design System: Deimos

## 1. Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Mandate Room"**

Deimos structures the capital behind infrastructure, energy, real assets, sports, hospitality, and PPP/concession deals that aren't yet investable — this site should read like the room where that structuring happens, not like a pitch deck advertising it. The register is navy-led institutional gravity in the vein of Houlihan Lokey and Lazard: sober hierarchy, disciplined whitespace, and a premium transaction-advisory tone. Motion is choreographed but understated — slow, deliberate scroll sequences are earned only when they communicate scale, jurisdictional reach, or transaction complexity; everywhere else, motion stays restrained and functional.

This system explicitly rejects the generic AI-SaaS/startup landing-page register: Linear-copycat dark gradients, Notion-style productivity minimalism, Webflow template card grids, pastel gradients, oversized rounded cards, floating dashboard mockups, playful icons, and "build faster with AI" energy. Deimos should not look like a software startup, a crypto project, a VC-fund clone, or a luxury black-and-gold concierge brand.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Navy-led institutional palette carrying real surface weight, not a token accent
- Serif editorial gravity in headlines, disciplined sans everywhere functional
- Motion as evidence of scale (jurisdiction, metrics, deal complexity), not decoration
- Whitespace and hierarchy do the persuading; ornament does none of it

## 2. Colors

**The Committed Rule.** Navy is not an accent here — it carries 30–60% of the surface. The palette is deliberately narrow: deep navy, ink blue, slate navy, cool grey, off-white, muted steel blue, and desaturated cobalt. No gold, no bright SaaS blue, no purple gradients, no cream/startup neutrals, no neon, no pastels.

## 3. Typography

**Display Font:** `[serious editorial serif or refined display face — to be chosen at implementation]`
**Body Font:** `[clean institutional sans — to be chosen at implementation]`

**Character:** Serif display + sans body. The pairing should feel like high-end private-capital / investment-banking material — a serif carrying editorial gravity in headlines and section statements, a disciplined sans carrying body copy, navigation, metrics, and UI. Never a single geometric sans doing both jobs; never two similar sans faces paired together.

### Hierarchy
- **Display** (serif, large scale, tight but not cramped): section-opening statements and hero headlines — the moments the "Mandate Room" tone needs to land.
- **Body** (sans, restrained size, 65–75ch max line length): all functional reading — descriptions, case narratives, disclosures.
- **Label** (sans, smaller scale, likely slight tracking): metrics, navigation, data labels — precision-forward, not decorative.

### Named Rules
**The One-Serif Rule.** Only the display role ever uses the serif. Every functional or data-adjacent element stays in the sans — mixing them elsewhere reads as inconsistent rather than deliberate.

## 4. Elevation

Layered, but understated: depth should read as tonal shifts within the navy family (slate navy vs. ink blue vs. off-white surfaces), not as drop shadows. Choreographed motion sequences may introduce depth through parallax or reveal staging rather than shadow elevation — the "cinematic" quality comes from motion and imagery, not from card-lifting.

### Named Rules
**The No-Card-Lift Rule.** Depth is never communicated by lifting a card off a flat background with a shadow. Use tonal surface shifts instead.

## 6. Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** let deep navy carry real surface weight (30–60%), per the Committed color strategy.
- **Do** pair a serious editorial serif (display only) with a disciplined institutional sans (everything else).
- **Do** reserve choreographed, scroll-driven motion for moments that communicate scale, jurisdictional reach, or transaction complexity — otherwise keep motion restrained and functional.
- **Do** use tonal navy-family layering for depth, not shadow-based card elevation.
- **Do** aim for the register of Houlihan Lokey, Lazard, or a sovereign/infrastructure investment presentation: sober hierarchy, disciplined whitespace, cinematic aerial/jurisdictional imagery used sparingly and seriously.

### Don't:
- **Don't** use gold, brass, or warm-metallic luxury-concierge accents.
- **Don't** use bright SaaS blues, purple gradients, cream/startup neutrals, neon colors, or playful pastels.
- **Don't** default to the generic AI-SaaS/startup landing page: no Linear-style dark gradients, no Notion-style productivity minimalism, no Webflow template card grids, no oversized rounded cards, no floating dashboard mockups, no playful icons, no "build faster with AI" energy.
- **Don't** use bouncy easing, playful micro-interactions, excessive scroll-jacking, or decorative animation.
- **Don't** use gradient text, side-stripe card borders, identical icon-grid feature sections, or a tiny uppercase eyebrow above every section — these read as templated regardless of register.
- **Don't** let this read as a crypto project, VC-fund clone, or software startup product page.
